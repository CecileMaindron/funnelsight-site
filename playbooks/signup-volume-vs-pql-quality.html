More sign-ups or better conversion: where your next customers come from

Updated November 23, 2026

To win more customers, the usual reflex is to add at the top of the funnel: another channel, more paid budget, more campaigns, a shorter form or a lower MQL bar. Growth needs some of that. But the steps after the first visit can bring as many customers, often for less money, through in-house work and small changes to a page, a setup flow or an onboarding email.

Where 10% more customers can come from

More customers don't have to start with more traffic or more sign-ups. Each step of a trial funnel converts at its own rate, and a gain at any of them carries down to customers.

Lead volume vs lead quality

Sometimes the change that brings the volume is what lowers the rates. A SaaS team with a free trial cuts its sign-up form from six fields to three, removing phone number, company and job title. Sign-ups go up 10%, and the quarterly report calls it a win. Followed down to the customers sales signed, the same funnel tells another story.

A shorter form lowers the bar to entry, so the extra sign-ups include more people who just want to look. And without a phone number, reps reached fewer of the PQLs. Lowering the PQL bar or blaming lead quality wouldn't fix it: the audience and the bar are the same, and what changed is the information reps relied on.

When doing more works against you

More campaigns to the same audience, more emails and more webinars, wear it out: open and attendance rates drop, and so does the response to the next campaign. This audience fatigue rarely shows in a report built on volume. More leads also mean less time per lead for sales. And many changes at once leave a funnel no one can read: when results move, no one knows which change did it.

Where to look first

Start with the step that loses the most against its usual rate, not the most visible one. Change one thing at a time, date it, and read the step after the one it was meant to improve once enough time has passed: see how long each change takes to show up.

What to watch after each change
What you changeWhat it can improveWhat to watch next
A shorter sign-up formMore sign-upsPQLs and leads accepted by sales per sign-up, and whether you can still reach users who stop being active
A new channel or audienceMore sign-ups, often cheaperThe cost per lead accepted by sales, and how that audience converts compared with your usual one
A clearer page or call to actionMore sign-ups from the same visitorsWhether the new sign-ups set up and activate like the others
A change to onboardingMore activated trialsTrial-to-paid of those trials, and how long they took to activate
A lower PQL barMore PQLs for salesHow the PQLs that only clear the new bar convert, and the time reps spend on each one

In Funnelsight, each step is read by source down to revenue, and every change stays dated. A gain at one step shows up in the steps below it, and a lowered threshold shows up as a lowered threshold, not as an unexplained dip a few weeks later.

Does this mean the sign-up form should never change?

No. Testing fields can help. Change one field at a time, leave it long enough to read the result, and check its effect down to the leads sales accepts, not just the sign-up count.
