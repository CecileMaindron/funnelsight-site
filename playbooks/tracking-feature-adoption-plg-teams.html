What feature adoption doesn't tell you

Tracking feature adoption works like measuring channels. It shows where to invest, what to develop and what to stop, and as with channels, volume and frequency of use don't say enough on their own.

Three questions usage can't answer alone

What feature adoption answers once it's read with acquisition, sales and billing data
QuestionRead usage withWhat it helps decide
What brought people to the trial?Their acquisition source and what they did firstWhich features to put forward in campaigns and on the site
What convinced them to pay?Trial outcomes in the CRMWhere onboarding should lead, and what belongs in the trial
What keeps them, and grows what they pay?Renewals, churn and billingWhat to protect and develop, and what can stop

A feature's role isn't always the one it was built for. Some are must-haves, because they carry the value proposition or keep the product level with competitors. Others turn out to keep customers when they were expected to convert them, or the reverse. The answers also differ by persona, job to be done or company segment, so it's worth reading each question segment by segment before deciding.

Read account by account, the same usage data also shows which customers are growing and which ones are drifting away (see expansion or churn: reading the signals in an account).
