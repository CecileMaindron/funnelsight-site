Activation and retention dashboard for PLG teams

Funnelsight puts activation and retention on the same screen, built around the activation event your team defines, not a generic default. You see which sources bring users who get value from the trial, how each week's sign-ups are doing, and which customers need attention.

Questions it answers

Which sources bring users who activate, not just sign up?

Sign-up volume says which channels are loud. The dashboard follows each source through the trial, to the users who reached the activation event and the ones who became paying customers. To compare sources on the revenue they bring and the time they take to cover their cost, see revenue and payback by source.

Is this week's cohort activating more slowly than the last?

Each week's sign-ups form a cohort. The share that activates should stay fairly stable from one week to the next, so a drop stands out, and Funnelsight flags it when it falls outside the range your team sets. A dated note on that week keeps the explanation next to the number.

Without the note, the dip would be debated at the next review. When a cohort drops, splitting it by source and checking time to value tells you whether the cause is who signed up or what happened next.

Do larger companies behave differently in the trial?

The same cohorts can be filtered by segment, for example by company size when a team starts targeting larger accounts, or by country when it opens a new market. It shows both how many sign-ups come from those accounts and whether they use the trial differently.

Which customers are slipping, and which are ready to expand?

After the trial, the same view follows paying accounts. Rules your team sets flag a drop in usage or an account approaching its plan limit, and the alert reaches the team by email or in the CRM it already works from.

How it works

You set the activation event that matters for your product, a specific action, not a generic template. That definition then applies to every report in Funnelsight, so activation means the same thing whether someone is looking at a cohort view or a single account. If you're still piecing activation metrics together from spreadsheets and product logs, this replaces that manual step.

"Activated" means the event your team chose, not a login. For a reporting tool, it might be opening a dashboard several times in a week. For a data pipeline, it might be the first sync that runs on its own, after which people only log in to fix or change something. The same choice applies later, when your churn-risk rules decide what counts as an active account.

When the definition changes, the change is logged, and past periods are either recalculated or the break is clearly marked. Each team builds the views it needs on the same numbers: weekly detail for the people running onboarding, a monthly summary for leadership.

Data updates on a schedule you control, hourly or on a cadence that fits your team. Setup doesn't require a data team. You can start by importing a spreadsheet to test the dashboard before connecting a live source, and have something working the same day. Grouped by account, the same view shows when several people from one company are trying the product.

What it does, and what it doesn't

It does

  • One activation event, defined once and applied to every report
  • Activation and trial conversion by acquisition source
  • Weekly cohorts, with dated notes on the periods that need explaining
  • Churn-risk and expansion flags, on rules your team sets and can see
  • Alerts by email or in your CRM
  • Start from a spreadsheet, connect live sources later

It doesn't

  • Stream data in real time: it refreshes on a schedule you choose
  • Predict from a model nobody can read: every flag comes from a visible rule
  • Replace your product analytics tool: Funnelsight reads from it
  • Model custom data or sync a data warehouse: that still needs technical help

Is the dashboard updated in real time?

No. Data refreshes on a schedule you set, for example every hour, rather than as a continuous stream. A set cadence also keeps the numbers stable while a team discusses them.

Do I need to define activation separately for each report?

No. You define your activation event once, and it applies across all reports and views in Funnelsight.

Can I see activation and conversion by acquisition source?

Yes. Each source shows its sign-ups, the share that activated during the trial and the share that became paying customers, so a channel isn't judged on sign-ups alone.
