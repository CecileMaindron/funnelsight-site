Pipeline coverage ratio: how much pipeline marketing needs, by channel

Updated November 25, 2026

Pipeline coverage ratio compares your pipeline with the revenue you need to close. With a $1M target and 3x coverage, you need $3M of pipeline. For marketing, the harder question comes next: which channels will create it, with the budget you have.

How to calculate pipeline coverage ratio

Formula

Pipeline coverage ratio = pipeline ÷ revenue target for the same period

Sales teams use it to check the open pipeline against the quarter's quota. Marketing uses it the other way round, to turn a revenue target into the pipeline it has to create: pipeline needed = revenue target × coverage.

The coverage you need comes from your win rate: divide 1 by it. If you win one opportunity in three, 3x is enough. If you win one in four, you need 4x. That's why 3x to 4x is the usual range in B2B, and why the figure moves when the win rate does, for example when a team starts selling to enterprise.

Plan the pipeline by channel

Start from what each channel did last year: what it cost, and the pipeline its leads created. Dividing one by the other gives the pipeline each dollar brought, the base of the plan. Change it where you expect a channel to do better or worse this year, set this year's spend, and see whether the plan reaches the target.

Shared costs come off the budget before the split. The team, the tools and the content used by several channels would be paid whatever the mix, so spreading them across channels would change each channel's rate without changing what the plan can buy.

Where last year's pipeline by channel comes from

The pipeline a channel created depends on how you credit it. Before asking for a demo, a buyer may see a LinkedIn ad, read a blog article, join a webinar, then search for your name. Marketing attribution decides how much of the opportunity each of these touches gets. The rule you choose is called an attribution model.

Some models give all the credit to a single touch: the first one (first touch) or the last one before the request (last touch, also called last click). Multi-touch attribution shares it, equally between all touches (linear) or with more weight on the first and the last (U-shaped, also called position-based).

No model is right or wrong. First touch shows which channels bring in new buyers. Last touch shows which ones trigger the request. In B2B, where an opportunity takes several touches over weeks or months, U-shaped is a good default: it credits the channel that started the path and the one that closed it, without ignoring the rest.

For the plan, keep last year's model and attribution window, the period over which touches are counted. Under last touch, the LinkedIn ad in the table created no pipeline at all; under U-shaped, it created $10k. A change of rule between two years would look like a change in results.

What is a good pipeline coverage ratio?

Between 3x and 4x is the usual range in B2B, but the right figure comes from your win rate: 1 divided by the share of opportunities you win. A team that wins one in three needs 3x; one that wins one in five needs 5x. Coverage far above that often means the pipeline holds opportunities that won't close.

Weighted or unweighted pipeline coverage?

Unweighted coverage adds up the full value of every open opportunity. Weighted coverage multiplies each one by its chance of closing, often based on its stage. To plan the pipeline marketing has to create, use unweighted coverage with your win rate. Weighted coverage is more useful to check whether the current quarter will close.

Does Funnelsight calculate CAC payback by channel?

Yes, when finance shares gross margin or you agree on which costs to count. Payback divides what a channel cost by the monthly recurring revenue its customers brought. It reads past results and is mostly used by finance; planning starts from pipeline, as on this page.

ROAS vs ROI: what's the difference?

ROAS (return on ad spend) divides the revenue from your ads by what they cost: $300,000 for $100,000 of ads is a ROAS of 3. ROI looks at what you earned beyond the cost, and can include every cost of the channel, not only the ads. The same campaign has an ROI of 200%. In B2B, ad platforms usually see leads, not signed contracts. Use ROAS to compare ads, and ROI from your CRM to compare channels.

Can we include costs that no ad platform holds?

Yes. Partner commissions, events or content production can be imported from a spreadsheet or entered by hand. A channel with no cost entered counts as zero, so it's worth checking which channels still have none.
