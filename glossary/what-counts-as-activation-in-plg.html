What counts as user activation in product-led growth

Activation is the moment a new user first gets real value from the product, when it does the job they came for. It isn't the moment they create an account. Sign-up and first login are the usual defaults, because they are easy to count. Neither tells you whether the product actually worked for that person.

How to calculate the activation rate

Once the activation event is chosen, the calculation is simple:

Formula

Activation rate (%) = (number of users who completed the activation event ÷ total new sign-ups) × 100

Teams rarely disagree on the formula. They disagree on the event in the numerator.

Why track activation

Before a trial can convert, the user has to use the product. A low activation rate is an early warning, weeks before conversion figures are in. The message may have brought the wrong people, the product may not fit that segment, or onboarding may lose users before their first useful result.

Activation doesn't guarantee a sale. Some customers buy without touching the trial, and some contracts renew with little use. But users who never activate are much less likely to pay or stay.

How to define activation for your product

Definitions vary from one company to the next, and some teams use the word for the whole path from sign-up to regular use. This page uses the most common meaning, the first time the product does its job for the user. It helps to place it between what comes before and what comes after:

To choose the event, ask what your product can't do for anyone without it, and pick the first time it does that, end to end. Every new user should be able to reach it in the first days, whatever they came for. Then check it against the users who left. If those who reach it don't stay more often than the others, look for a better event. Wes Bush's ProductLed Playbook breaks this down further if you want the full framework.

Where activation numbers go wrong

No one owns the step. Product counts an event in its analytics tool. Marketing reports trial-to-paid and skips the step between sign-up and payment. Sales sees new trials in the CRM, when it sees them at all. Activation ends up with several definitions, or none, and the one written in a slide is never revisited.

Activation becomes the sales signal. Reaching the activation event shows the product worked for someone, not that they're ready to buy or that a call would help. Many convinced users buy on their own, and some have no budget at all. A product qualified lead adds what activation doesn't show: an account that fits your target, and signs of buying intent.

The definition changes without a date. A feature moves, onboarding changes, a new plan adds a different first step, and the event that made sense a year ago no longer fits. Revisit it when the product changes, not every time the rate dips. When it does dip, time to value helps tell a change in who signs up from a product that got harder. Date each change, so the break isn't read as a trend.

Is activation the same as completing onboarding?

No. Onboarding is wider. It should get users to activation, then help them use more of the product. If many users start onboarding but never activate, the flow needs rework. Some skip it and activate anyway, which is fine.

What's the difference between activation and adoption?

Activation happens once, the first time a new user gets real value from the product, often during the trial. Adoption comes after. It describes how widely and how regularly the product, or one of its features, is used over time. The activation rate tells you whether new users reach that first value, and the adoption rate whether they keep coming back to it.

How soon after sign-up should activation happen?

Ideally in the first session, or at least in the first few days. A self-serve trial assumes the product is simple enough to start alone. When activation takes longer, the user is usually stuck in setup, waiting for access they don't have yet, or on a configuration that takes more than one session.

Do I need engineering help to track activation?

Not to define the event or follow it through the funnel in Funnelsight, if your product analytics tool already records the action. If it doesn't, recording it is a short, one-time setup, standard for any analytics tool.
