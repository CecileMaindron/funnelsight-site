What counts as user activation in product-led growth

Activation is the moment a new user experiences the value your product promised, not the moment they create an account. Signup and first login are the usual defaults, because they are easy to count. Neither tells you whether the product actually worked for that person.

Why "activation" resists a single definition

A project management tool and an analytics tool don't activate the same way. For one, activation might mean inviting a teammate and assigning a task. For the other, connecting a data source and viewing a first real report. No event counts as activation across every product, which is why so many teams end up tracking the wrong one by default.

Login counts are easy to pull from any system, so they become the fallback metric. On their own, they say almost nothing: a user can log in five times without ever touching the feature that makes your product worth paying for.

How to define activation for your product

Start from retention, not from your onboarding flow, and work backward:

  1. Start from the users who stayed

    Take the accounts that kept using the product and became customers, not the ones who finished onboarding.

  2. Find the action they share

    Look for the action, or short sequence of actions, that shows up consistently among them in their first days.

  3. Check it against the users who left

    A good activation event is rare among accounts that churned. If everyone does it, it doesn't separate anything.

  4. Prefer a repeated action to a single step

    A completed setup step is rarely enough. The first action that resembles everyday use of the product is usually a better signal, and feature adoption rate shows how to read each feature against revenue, from trial to renewal.

What this looks like for real products

Slack is the best-known example: its team found that almost all the teams that had exchanged around 2,000 messages kept using the product. The principle carries over: a strong activation event is something your best-retained accounts do repeatedly, not something everyone does once during signup. Wes Bush's ProductLed Playbook breaks this down further if you want the full framework.

Why teams end up with three activation numbers

A product manager tracks feature adoption in the analytics tool. A growth marketer builds cohort views in a spreadsheet from raw exports. Sales watches login frequency as a rough proxy for interest. None of these numbers agree. Activation then gets defined once, in a slide, and isn't revisited as the product changes.

Once a team agrees on one event, the calculation is simple:

Formula

Activation rate (%) = (number of users who completed the activation event ÷ total new signups) × 100

The formula isn't the hard part. Getting three tools to agree on the numerator is.

Keep the definition stable as the product changes

An activation event that made sense a year ago can drift: a feature moves, onboarding changes, a new plan adds a different first step. Revisit the definition when the product changes, not every time the rate dips. When the rate does dip, time to value helps tell a change in who signs up from a product that got harder. When you do change the definition, write down the date and the reason, and compare the old and new rates over the same weeks before reading a trend across the break.

Is activation the same as completing onboarding?

No. Onboarding completion measures whether someone finished a set of steps you designed. Activation measures whether they experienced real value. A user can finish onboarding and never activate.

How soon after signup should activation happen?

It depends on the product, and no fixed window applies everywhere. What matters more than timing is whether the event you chose actually separates the users who stay from the ones who leave.

Do I need engineering help to track activation?

Not to define the event or follow it through the funnel in Funnelsight, if your product analytics tool already records the action. If it doesn't, recording it is a short, one-time setup, standard for any analytics tool.
