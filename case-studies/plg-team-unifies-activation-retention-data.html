PLG team unifies activation and retention data

Illustrative example. The account below describes Taskframe, a fictional project management SaaS company created to show how Funnelsight works in practice. It is not a real customer, and the figures are illustrative, not measured results.

At a glance
  • Company: Taskframe, a project management tool (fictional)
  • Teams involved: growth, customer success and product
  • Problem: one word, "activation", used for three different things
  • What changed: two named definitions, one for trial users and one for paying accounts
  • Result (illustrative): planning meetings start from the numbers, and customer success focuses on accounts that are actually stuck

The situation

Taskframe sells a 14-day self-serve trial and annual plans signed with sales. Three teams used the word "activation" for three different things. Growth counted a trial user as activated once they created a first project, and speed mattered: a trial that activates on day twelve rarely converts. Customer success counted a paid account as activated once the team started working in it, which could come weeks after signature, because the customer was preparing its rollout, hiring someone to own the tool or waiting for an integrator. On an annual contract, three days or three weeks made little difference. Product counted active users, so an account where one manager used the tool every day looked healthy while the rest of the team never logged in.

The gap showed up at planning time. Each team brought its own number, and the meeting started by reconciling them instead of deciding anything.

What changed

Taskframe connected its product usage data and CRM data in a single funnel view, which put the three definitions side by side. Rather than force one of them on everyone, it kept two and named them. Trial activation is measured per user, within the trial, and timed. Account activation is measured per account, with at least three people completing a project, and tracked against the start date the customer planned. Retention is read on account activation. When an account waits for an integrator, a dated note says so, and customer success stops flagging it as at risk.

Setup didn't require a data team. The dashboard was live the same day, using a spreadsheet export while a live data source was being connected.

The result

In this illustrative scenario, planning meetings start from the numbers, and customer success spends its time on the accounts that are actually stuck rather than on the ones waiting to roll out. The conversation moved from "whose number is right" to "what do we do about it."
