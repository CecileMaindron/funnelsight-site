Cutting the marketing budget without emptying next year's pipeline

Mid-year, marketing had to spend less with the year's pipeline target unchanged. The channels first in line for cuts were the ones building next year's pipeline.

Queuedesk is a fictional company, and the figures are illustrative, not measured results.

  • 20%cut from the rest of the year's budget
  • $3.50 vs $1.80expected revenue per dollar, organic vs paid search
  • +5%activated trials (31% → 32.5%), after one change to the setup checklist
Company
Queuedesk, customer support software for mid-sized companies
Asked by
The CFO
Data used
Leads created three quarters earlier: cost, pipeline and win rate by channel
Channels
Paid search, content and SEO, partners, events, webinars

Halfway through the year, Queuedesk's CFO asked marketing to take 20% off the budget left for the remaining two quarters. The quarterly report compared channels on cost per lead, and the answer seemed obvious. Paid search brought the most leads at the lowest cost, so it stayed. Events and part of the content program cost the most per lead, so they would go.

Before deciding, the team looked back at the leads created three quarters earlier and compared what each channel had cost with the pipeline those leads had created and how often their deals were won. Each channel turned out to have its own profile.

Four channels, four profiles

Paid search fills the funnel fast, at a price. It brings the most leads and the most new contacts, and its deals close within a few months. Each deal costs more than for content, though, and paid search now carries 45% of new pipeline. A jump in bids or a change in an ad platform's rules would hit the next quarter directly.

Content is the cheapest channel and the slowest one. Its leads take half a year to sign, but each deal costs a fraction of the others. Cutting it would save money this year and show only next year, when this year's content leads would have signed. Rebuilding it afterwards would take longer still, since an audience grows slowly and drops quickly.

Partners sign fastest, and the biggest deals. The cost per deal includes the partner's commission, which takes a fifth of the first year's revenue. The channel is worth growing where that margin holds.

Events look like the worst channel, until the deals already in progress enter the picture. As a source of new leads, a trade show produces hundreds of badge scans and very few customers. But the CRM also records the meetings sales held there with accounts already in the pipeline. Opportunities with such a meeting closed in three months on average, against five for the others. The value of an event lies in those meetings, not in the badges.

Webinars stayed out of this comparison. They create few new leads, and their audience is mostly existing prospects and customers. At almost no cost, they keep that audience informed.

What the team decided

  • TightenPaid searchCost-per-lead plan: keep as is

    Its average hid its weakest spend: broad keywords and countries without a sales team brought leads that rarely signed. Cutting those kept most of the qualified leads.

  • ProtectContent and SEOCost-per-lead plan: cut in part

    Much of next year's pipeline is being built by it now, and it would take longest to rebuild.

  • SelectEventsCost-per-lead plan: cut

    Queuedesk kept the two shows where its pipeline accounts go, and dropped the others.

  • GrowPartnersCost-per-lead plan: no change

    Where the margin after commission holds.

Fewer leads, so each one has to count

Spending less still meant fewer leads, against the same target for the year. The team had to make up the difference without budget.

One step of the trial had been on the list for months. The setup checklist opened with connecting the company's support inbox, which often needs IT, while the test address every trial gets was further down. Trials stalled on the first step. The team moved the test request to the top, so a trial could answer its first conversation right away. In the four weeks that followed, trials that answered a first conversation, the team's activation event, rose from 31% to 32.5%. Trial-to-paid will be readable after one full trial.

Some paid work moved to free channels. A LinkedIn campaign gave way to posts from the founder, shared by employees through the advocacy program. One of the dropped trade shows was replaced by a webinar for the same accounts.

The 20% was found, and the content building next year's pipeline was protected. Fewer leads are coming this year, so the plan counts on more of them becoming customers.

What to copy

  • Judge a channel on the pipeline it creates and the deals it wins, not on its cost per lead.
  • Count open deals at the channel's win rate. The slowest channels look worst before their deals sign.
  • Judge events on the open deals they move forward, not on the leads they create.
  • Look past a channel's average and cut the spend that brings the least pipeline.
  • With less budget for the same target, work on what costs time rather than money: the step where trials stall, the organic version of a paid campaign.

See what each channel brings, and how fast, before you cut anything.
