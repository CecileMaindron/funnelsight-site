Your most-used feature isn't the one that keeps customers

Rankloop's site audit brings in most of its trials. A year later, those trials bring in the least revenue.

Rankloop is a fictional company, and the figures are illustrative, not measured results.

  • +12%new monthly revenue from trials, the following quarter
  • +150trials a quarter starting from rank tracking
  • 1 in 3agencies arriving through the audit now build a client report during the trial
Company
Rankloop, an SEO tool for agencies and in-house marketing teams, with a 14-day trial and no card required
Asked by
Product and marketing, for the quarterly feature review
Data used
One quarter of trials, followed for a year: entry page, conversion, plan, renewals
Entry points
Site audit, rank tracking, client reporting

Rankloop had built everything around its site audit: the ads, the landing pages, the onboarding. It worked. The audit brought most of the trials, and 92% of trial users ran it. In the quarterly review, it was the obvious feature to keep pushing.

When the whole journey leads to one feature, though, almost everyone uses it. So the team grouped trials by the feature that brought them in, and used Funnelsight to follow each group to revenue a year later.

From the most trials to the least revenue

The audit attracts the most, and keeps the least. Many who come for it want one job done: find what's wrong with a site, and fix it. They convert less often, pick the smallest plan, and leave once the fixes are in.

Client reporting converts best, for a small audience. Agencies that come to send monthly reports to their clients convert at 38%, on a plan three times the price, and they stay. But they bring 250 trials a quarter. Moving the budget to them would buy a higher rate and fewer customers.

Rank tracking sits in between, and people search for it. It converts better than the audit, its customers stay, and unlike client reporting, it has search demand to grow into.

What the team decided

  • KeepSite audit

    Still the main door. It brings the most customers in number.

  • OpenRank tracking

    A landing page and search campaigns of its own, instead of a line on the audit page.

  • RouteAgencies in the trial

    Trial users who manage sites for clients are shown, on day one, how to turn their audit into a client report.

In this illustrative scenario, the following quarter brought 150 more trials through rank tracking, and new monthly revenue from trials rose 12%. The overall conversion rate barely moved: the gain came from the mix of trials. How long the new customers stay will show in a year.

What to copy

  • Group trials by what brought them in before judging a feature by its usage. A feature the whole journey points to will always look popular.
  • Compare entry points on the revenue still there a year later. The best conversion rate can come with the smallest audience.
  • Before moving budget to a feature that converts well, check how many people want it and whether they search for it.
